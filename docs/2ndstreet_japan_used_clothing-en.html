卖旧衣服的 2nd Street，为什么在日本门店比优衣库还多？

Why Does Secondhand-Clothing Chain 2nd Street Have More Stores in Japan Than Uniqlo?

This episode breaks down 2nd Street, the Japanese secondhand-clothing chain that runs over 900 stores across Japan and more than 1,000 worldwide, outpacing Uniqlo's Japan count. The winning formula combines three things: a country where the idle-economy soil is deep, parent company GEO's nationwide store network, and a human-buyer pricing system. Together they turned apparel — the hardest category to standardize — into a scale-model direct-operated chain that few rivals have matched.

避坑 PITFALLS

✕ The store is very many, more than Uniqlo.
✓ It has more stores in Japan than Uniqlo does.

中文式「很很多」直译不成立；英文用 more … than 比较结构，或动词 outnumber / outpace。

✕ They buy second-hand clothes from consumers and sell them.
✓ It buys used clothes from consumers, processes them, and resells them.

作为商业流程描述，补上 processing 环节信息更完整；resell 比重复 sell 更地道。

✕ The clothing is very hard to standardize.
✓ Apparel is one of the hardest categories to standardize.

直译「服装很难标准化」缺少对比语境；用 one of the hardest categories 表达相对难度更贴切。

✕ GEO knows appraisal needs people, but still chooses clothes.
✓ GEO knew appraisal depends on human experience, yet chose apparel precisely because those barriers mean less competition.

只译「需要人」丢掉了关键的「门槛高所以竞争少」因果逻辑；要补上理由才能表达原意。

认知转变 MINDSET SHIFTS

以为二手服装店是低端、难规模化的生意 → 直营+买手定价+全国网络，能让最难标准化的服装做成千家连锁
以为门店多就等于利润高 → 门店数超过优衣库，利润却不到其十分之一——规模不等于盈利
以为标准化是规模化的唯一前提 → 高门槛品类反而竞争更少，GEO 故意选最难做的服饰品类
